I started in geology at UC Santa Cruz because I love the outdoors, collecting field data on rock
orientation to work out the shape of folds. The part I liked most turned out to be analyzing the
data. In my last semester I learned MATLAB to analyze seismic data, and that got me into coding.

From there I did a master's in analytics at Northeastern, partly to help with my family's business,
and learned software engineering at a startup. Now I'm finishing a master's in computer science at
Northeastern and building AI tools with experts in other fields: a research assistant for a
postdoctoral biochemistry researcher, and a data extraction pipeline for scientists at Oak Ridge
National Laboratory.

Earth science wasn't a detour. It taught me to work with messy real-world data, which is pretty much
what I still do, just with research papers instead of rocks.
